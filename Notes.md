## GAAP Principals to Apply on Transactions

Consistency: By following standardized principles, GAAP ensures that financial statements are consistent from one reporting period to the next.
Comparability: With GAAP, stakeholders can compare financial statements across different companies or industries, making informed investment or business decisions.
Transparency: GAAP improves the reliability of financial information by requiring full disclosure of relevant financial details.
Legal Compliance: Adherence to GAAP is often mandated by regulators, lenders, and investors, ensuring organizations comply with financial reporting standards.
Improved Decision-Making: Accurate financial reporting under GAAP allows business owners and managers to analyze their organization’s performance and make sound strategic decisions.

## Double Entry Accounting

Every debit entry has a corresponding credit entry, helping keep the balance sheet balanced.
Assets=Liabilities+Equity

## Importance

Accuracy: Because the sum of all debits must always equal the sum of all credits, the system acts as a built-in check and balance.

Transparency: It creates a reliable audit trail, making it much easier to spot errors and prevent fraud.

Comprehensive Reporting: It allows businesses to easily generate crucial financial statements like the income statement and balance sheet.

## Key Principles of Canadian GAAP

Revenue Recognition Principle: Businesses recognize revenue only when it is earned and measurable. For example, they record revenue when they render a service or deliver a product, not when they receive payment.

Matching Principle: Businesses record expenses in the same period as the revenues they generate, ensuring an accurate representation of profitability.

Full Disclosure Principle: Companies disclose all relevant financial information that may impact users’ decisions in the financial statements.

Cost Principle: Businesses record assets at their historical cost rather than current market value, providing a reliable and objective basis for financial reporting.

Conservatism Principle: Accountants exercise caution when faced with uncertainty, ensuring they do not understate liabilities and expenses or overstate assets and revenues.

## Canadian GAAp vs IFRS

IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards)

Used by publicly accountable enterprises.
Principles-based approach, emphasizing judgment and interpretation.
Designed for global comparability.
ASPE (Accounting Standards for Private Enterprises)

Used by private enterprises in Canada.
Simplified rules compared to IFRS, reducing complexity for smaller businesses.
Focuses on cost-effective reporting for entities without external investors.
Both frameworks follow GAAP principles, with IFRS providing more detail and complexity, while ASPE suits smaller-scale operations
